Justice for Victims

Ending the culture of cover-ups. Victims of grooming gangs, rape, and institutional failure must be heard, believed, and protected — full stop. No more silencing, no more delays, no more excuses.

A systemic betrayal of victims

For decades, victims of child sexual exploitation across Britain raised their voices and were ignored. Girls as young as eleven were dismissed as troublemakers, promiscuous, or complicit. Police forces, social services, and local councils were warned — repeatedly — and chose to look away. In Rotherham, at least 1,400 children were abused over sixteen years. In Rochdale, in Telford, in Oldham, in town after town, the pattern repeated.

The failures were not accidents. They were choices. Choices made by institutions that placed their own reputations, political relationships, and fears of being called racist above their legal duty to protect children. Victims paid the price. Many are still paying it.

Sherrie De Kiguchi has worked frontline with a charity supporting rape survivors. She has sat with women who were failed at every turn — by the police who did not pursue their reports, by the courts that doubted their testimony, by the systems that were supposed to protect them. She does not campaign on this issue from the outside. She campaigns because she has seen what justice denied looks like.

"These women were failed once when it happened to them. We cannot fail them a second time by refusing to demand the truth and deliver the justice they are owed."

Inspired by Maggie Oliver

Maggie Oliver resigned from Greater Manchester Police in 2012 to become a whistleblower and survivor advocate after the force failed to prosecute the Rochdale grooming gang and dismissed the evidence she had gathered. Her decision cost her her career. She made it anyway.

Maggie Oliver's courage is what inspired Sherrie De Kiguchi to speak publicly. When institutions fail, individuals must step forward. Sherrie has done that — and she will keep doing it until justice is no longer the exception for survivors, but the expectation.

What Sherrie is campaigning for

  • Full national inquiry with teeth — an independent, statutory inquiry with the power to compel testimony and impose consequences on individuals and institutions that obstructed justice.
  • Named accountability — the officials, police officers, social workers, and politicians who suppressed reports and ignored victims must be named and, where there is evidence of criminal conduct, prosecuted.
  • Lifetime support for survivors — therapeutic, legal and practical support for CSE survivors, funded by the state, not dependent on postcode or charity capacity.
  • Reform of reporting systems — making it significantly easier and safer for victims to report abuse without fear of being disbelieved, criminalised or retraumatised by the process.
  • No gagging of whistleblowers — officers, social workers and public servants who come forward with evidence of institutional failure must be protected by law, not punished.
