Child Protection

Zero tolerance for grooming gangs. Full accountability for every institution, official and officer who knew what was happening to children in Rotherham, Rochdale, Telford and beyond — and looked the other way.

The scale of the failure

Child sexual exploitation by organised grooming gangs is one of the gravest failures of the British state in modern times. In Rotherham alone, the Jay Report found that at least 1,400 children had been abused over more than a decade. The abuse was known about. Warnings were raised. And they were ignored — deliberately, repeatedly, and at the highest levels of local government, social services and policing.

Across Britain, similar patterns played out in Rochdale, Telford, Oldham, Oxford, Newcastle, Bristol, and dozens of other towns. Victims were dismissed. Perpetrators were protected. Whistleblowers were silenced. The institutions whose whole purpose was to safeguard children chose instead to safeguard their own reputations.

"Every single child who was abused while officials looked away deserved better. They still deserve better. And the people who failed them must be held to account — not transferred, not retired, not quietly moved on."

Why Sherrie campaigns on this

Sherrie De Kiguchi is a former frontline charity worker who supported rape survivors. She has sat across from women who were abused as children, who reported what was happening, who were told they would not be believed — and who then lived with the consequences of that decision for the rest of their lives.

She is also a parish councillor. She understands local government and local accountability from the inside. And she knows that the failures in Rotherham were not unique failures — they were possible because of a culture that is still present in many institutions: a culture that protects the organisation before it protects the child.

This is not a party political issue for Sherrie. It is a moral one. And she will not stop raising it until real, structural change is in place.

What Sherrie is campaigning for

  • Mandatory national inquiry into all known grooming gang operations — with the power to name individuals, compel testimony, and refer for prosecution where evidence of criminal cover-up exists.
  • Criminal liability for institutional cover-up — senior officials who knowingly suppressed reports of child abuse should face criminal charges, not comfortable pension arrangements.
  • Grooming gang register — a national database of convicted CSE perpetrators, accessible to safeguarding professionals, equivalent to the sex offenders register.
  • Compulsory CSE training for all front-line public sector workers — police, social workers, teachers, and NHS staff must be equipped and legally required to act on signs of exploitation.
  • Survivor-led reform — survivors and their advocates must have formal, meaningful input into the design of safeguarding systems. They are the experts. They should be treated as such.
  • Independent oversight of social services — an end to self-regulation of the same departments that failed children for years.
